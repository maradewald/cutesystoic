# Cutesy Stoic — Quote Bank v1

## Sourcing rules

All quotes below come from public-domain translations:

- **Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations*** — tr. George Long, 1862. Use **Project
  Gutenberg #15877**, *Thoughts of Marcus Aurelius Antoninus*. ⚠️ Gutenberg
  **#2680** is titled *Meditations* but is **Meric Casaubon's 1634**
  translation — different wording entirely. Do not use it for Long quotes.
- **Epictetus, *Enchiridion*** — tr. Elizabeth Carter, 1758. Source used:
  Wikisource, *All the Works of Epictetus, Which Are Now Extant* (1759 ed.),
  "The Enchiridion, or Manual." ⚠️ Gutenberg **#45109** (*The Enchiridion*) is
  **Higginson's**, and Gutenberg **#10661** is **Long's** Epictetus — both PD,
  both worded differently. Carter says "in our **power**," never "in our
  control."
- **Seneca, *Letters to Lucilius*** — tr. Richard Gummere, Loeb vols. 1–3
  (1917 / 1920 / 1925 — all pre-1929, PD in the US). Source used: Wikisource,
  *Moral letters to Lucilius*. Not on Gutenberg.
- **Seneca, *On Providence*** — tr. Aubrey Stewart, 1889, in *Minor Dialogues*
  (Gutenberg **#64576**). Do **not** use Basore (1932) or any modern translation.

**Verification status:** ✅ **Full pass completed 2026-08-17.** Every line below
was checked against the source texts above, character by character, and
corrected in place where it drifted. Boxes are ticked. Wording below is now the
translator's, not a paraphrase — do not "clean it up" before posting. The
archaic voice (thee/thou, semicolons for days, 18th-c. capitalisation
normalised) is a feature; it plays the "seriously" half of the brand.

**Known viral-quote traps** (do not post as-is):
- "The obstacle is the way" — modern book title; the passage is *Meditations*
  5.20, and Long's wording is very different (see ADVERSITY, #39).
- "Luck is what happens when preparation meets opportunity" — not Seneca.
- "We have two ears and one mouth…" — attributed to Zeno via Diogenes
  Laertius, not Epictetus; park it unless we source it properly.
- "A gem cannot be polished without friction…" — **cut from this bank**, see
  Rejected below.

Format: quote — reference — [x] verified. Notes only where there's a hook
worth remembering, or where the verification changed something.

## CONTROL (the department of what is ours)

1. "Of things, some are in our power, and others not." — Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 1 (Carter) — [x]
   - The founding text. Candidate for post 001.
   - **CORRECTED.** Was "Some things are in our control and others not" — that
     is a modern rendering, not Carter. Carter's word is **power**, and the
     sentence opens "Of things." Worth knowing: "control" is the tell that a
     stoic account is quoting a copyrighted translation.
2. "Men are disturbed, not by things, but by the principles and notions which they form concerning things." — Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 5 (Carter) — [x]
3. "Require not things to happen as you wish; but wish them to happen as they do happen, and you will go on well." — Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 8 (Carter) — [x]
   - Verified exact. (The Wikisource scan OCRs "wish" as "with" twice; the
     printed Carter reads "wish." Ignore the scan artifact.)
4. "Every thing hath two handles; the one, by which it may be borne; the other, by which it cannot." — Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 43 (Carter) — [x]
   - Extremely drawable. A cute object with two literal handles.
   - **CORRECTED.** Was "Everything has two handles… may be carried." Carter
     is "hath" and "borne." The archaism is the good version anyway.
5. "If thou art pained by any external thing, it is not this thing that disturbs thee, but thy own judgment about it. And it is in thy power to wipe out this judgment now." — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 8.47 (Long) — [x]
   - **CORRECTED.** One word: "it is not this **thing** that disturbs thee."
6. "Take away thy opinion, and then there is taken away the complaint, 'I have been harmed.'" — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 4.7 (Long) — [x]
7. "Remember that you are an actor in a drama, of such a kind as the author pleases to make it." — Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 17 (Carter) — [x]

## FEAR, WORRY & IMAGINATION

29. "We suffer more often in imagination than in reality." — Seneca, *Letters* 13 (Gummere) — [x]
    - The most viral-friendly line in the bank, and it's actually real, actually
      Gummere, actually PD. Verified word for word. Use early.
30. "There are more things, Lucilius, likely to frighten us than there are to crush us." — Seneca, *Letters* 13 (Gummere) — [x]
    - **CORRECTED.** Gummere keeps the direct address: "more things, Lucilius,
      likely…". Keeping it makes it read as a letter, which is the truth of the text.
31. "Do not disturb thyself by thinking of the whole of thy life." — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 8.36 (Long) — [x]
32. "For nowhere either with more quiet or more freedom from trouble does a man retire than into his own soul." — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 4.3 (Long) — [x]
    - **CORRECTED — this was the worst one.** The old wording ("Nowhere can man
      find a quieter or more untroubled retreat than in his own soul") is a

      copyright-adjacent mistake the bank exists to prevent.
    - Long's setup mocks people who seek country houses and sea-shores first:
      "it is in thy power whenever thou shalt choose to retire into thyself."
      Consider the longer setup in the caption.
33. "He who fears death either fears the loss of sensation or a different kind of sensation." — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 8.58 (Long) — [x]

## ADVERSITY

39. "The mind converts and changes every hindrance to its activity into an aid." — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 5.20 (Long) — [x]
    - This is the real "obstacle is the way" passage. Posting the PD wording
      under the viral one's shadow is a quietly superior move. Verified exact.
40. — **CUT.** See Rejected below.
41. "Fire tries gold, misfortune tries brave men." — Seneca, *On Providence* 5 (Stewart, 1889) — [x]
    - **SOURCED & CORRECTED.** Was "Fire tests gold, misfortune brave men" with
      no translation. Stewart's PD wording repeats the verb: "tries… tries."
42. "Our lack of confidence is not the result of difficulty; the difficulty comes from our lack of confidence." — Seneca, *Letters* 104.26 (Gummere) — [x]
    - **CORRECTED, and read this before using it.** The famous version ("It is
      not because things are difficult that we do not dare…") is a *different
      translation* of the same Latin — `non quia difficilia sunt non audemus,
      sed quia non audemus difficilia sunt` (Latin verified at Ep. 104.26). The
      citation was right; the English was not Gummere's.
    - Gummere's wording is flatter and loses the "dare." It may not survive as a
      graphic. Either post Gummere's honestly, or skip the line — do not post
      the punchy version under a Gummere credit.
43. "Look round at the courses of the stars, as if thou wert going along with them." — Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 7.47 (Long) — [x]

## Rejected

- ~~"A gem cannot be polished without friction, nor a man perfected without
  trials." — attributed to Seneca~~ — **APOCRYPHAL. CUT.** Not in the Letters,
  not in *Minor Dialogues*, not anywhere in the Seneca corpus. It is a Chinese
  proverb that circulates with a Seneca sticker on it; the earliest English
  appearances are 20th-century popular fiction. Do not post, even hedged.
