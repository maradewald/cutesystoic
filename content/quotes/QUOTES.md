## Sourcing rules

All quotes below come from public-domain translations:

- **Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations*** — tr. George Long, 1862 (Project Gutenberg / Wikisource)
- **Epictetus, *Enchiridion*** — tr. Elizabeth Carter, 1758 (widely mirrored; T.W. Higginson's 1865 revision also PD)
- **Seneca, *Letters to Lucilius*** — tr. Richard Gummere, Loeb vols. 1–3 (1917 / 1920 / 1925 — all pre-1929, PD in the US)
- Optional extra source if we want *On the Shortness of Life* later: Aubrey Stewart's 1889 *Minor Dialogues* (PD). Do **not** use Basore (1932) or any modern translation.

**Verification workflow (required):** wording below is from memory of the PD
translations and each line must be checked against the source text before it
ships — tick the box when verified. This is non-negotiable: half of Instagram
stoicism is misquoted or quietly lifted from copyrighted modern translations,
and we are not joining them. The archaic voice (thee/thou, semicolons for
days) is a feature — it plays the "seriously" half of the brand.

**Known viral-quote traps** (do not post as-is):
- "The obstacle is the way" — that's a modern book title; the underlying
  passage is *Meditations* 5.20, and Long's wording is very different (see
  ADVERSITY below).
- "Luck is what happens when preparation meets opportunity" — not Seneca.
- "We have two ears and one mouth…" — attributed to Zeno via Diogenes
  Laertius, not Epictetus; park it unless we source it properly.

Format: quote — reference — [ ] verified. Notes only where there's a hook
worth remembering.
