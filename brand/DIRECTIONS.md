# Cutesy Stoic — Brand Directions

Four genuinely distinct directions. Each is a complete system — type, palette,
motif language, caption voice — not a mood. Pick one; do not blend. The account
gets one identity. (Compare freely against whatever the other robot proposes;
these are built to be told apart at thumbnail size.)

Shared constants across all four:

- 1080×1350 feed posts, quote-forward, readable at thumbnail size
- The quote is always played straight. The cuteness never mocks the text;
  the juxtaposition does the comedy.
- Attribution on every post (author + work), small but present.

---

## Direction 1 — Chrome Tears

**Thesis:** Y2K gloss. Marcus Aurelius as a ringtone-era graphic. Liquid metal,
lens flare, jelly bevels. Memento mori, but it's an early-2000s CD single.

- **Palette:** silver chrome, glacier aqua `#7FD8E8`, hot pink `#FF5CA8`,
  deep space navy `#0B1030` grounds. Iridescent gradients allowed; earth tones
  banned.
- **Type:** Archivo Black or Bricolage Grotesque (heavy weights) with chrome /
  liquid treatments for the quote; Space Mono, tiny, for attribution — styled
  like a track listing.
- **Motifs:** chrome hearts, teardrops, orbiting sparkles, wireframe globes,
  Roman busts rendered as reflective metal. Butterflies permitted, sparingly.
- **Composition:** centered quote lockup, halo of motifs, heavy specular
  highlights. Feels like an album cover for a single called "Amor Fati."
- **Caption voice:** liner-notes sincere. "track 4. on wanting what happens."
- **Sample post:** "You could leave life right now." — chrome script arcing
  over a weeping metallic heart, aqua flare behind it.
- **Risk:** Y2K revival is crowded; execution has to be crisp or it reads as
  a Canva template from 2021.

## Direction 2 — The Sticker Drawer

**Thesis:** Ancient wisdom presented like a tiny reward from the 1991 sticker
drawer. The reference is 1980s/90s school-supply ephemera: simple drawings,
cheerful colors, paper backing, and tactile finishes. Sweet without being
infantile; nostalgic without becoming a costume. This is not generic kawaii.
The quote remains completely sincere.

- **Core visual grammar:** slightly wonky hand-drawn forms, uncomplicated black
  outlines, flat bright color, white die-cut borders, and ordinary objects or
  animals given tiny faces, arms, and personalities. The subjects may vary
  wildly; the drawing language stays fixed.
- **Palette:** warm sticker-backing white `#F3F0E8`, tomato red `#E44735`,
  school-bus yellow `#F2C94C`, pool blue `#58BFD1`, grass green `#59A65A`,
  and bubblegum pink `#E97BAA`. Use backing white plus no more than three
  brights per post. Fading, slight misregistration, and print texture are
  welcome; beige nostalgia filters are not.
- **Type:** the quote in a sober, bookish serif such as Century Schoolbook or
  Newsreader; attribution in a plain condensed grotesque, treated like a
  product code at the bottom of a sticker module. Avoid bubbly display type:
  the stickers carry the cute half of the brand.
- **Motifs:** original smiling school supplies, fruit, weather, household
  objects, animals, hands, stars, hearts, clouds, and small symbols of the
  passage. Philosophers do not appear as chibi mascots. No single franchise
  character is required.
- **Finish families:** flat printed paper is the default. Pearlescent, prismatic,
  glitter, flocked, or translucent finishes may define an occasional post or
  mini-series, but finishes are never mixed indiscriminately within one image.
- **Composition:** the post may resemble a complete sticker module, a page in
  an old sticker book, or one oversized reward sticker with a sober quote
  label. Preserve breathing room and thumbnail legibility. The account should
  feel collected over time, not decorated all at once.
- **Photography:** every sixth or ninth post may use a real purchased vintage
  sticker on Mara's face, hand, clothing, notebook, or an ordinary object.
  These photographs make the account physical and personal without requiring
  talking-to-camera content. Actual branded stickers are photographed as
  found; brand assets use original illustrations rather than copied designs.
- **Caption voice:** warm, plainspoken, and lightly deadpan. Never baby talk.
  \"you get a sticker for noticing.\" / \"today's sticker for coping
  appropriately.\"
- **Sample post:** \"Some things are in our control and others not.\" — a
  cheerful little umbrella sticker remains composed beneath three raindrops;
  the quote sits beside it in sober schoolbook type, with \"EPICTETUS ·
  ENCHIRIDION 1\" printed like the code at the foot of a vintage sticker sheet.
- **Do not blend:** no modern puffy 3D kawaii, chibi philosophers, Y2K chrome,
  airbrushed rainbow fantasy, snarky slogan buttons, or generic sticker-app
  iconography.
- **Risk:** nostalgia can collapse into reference dumping or imitation. The
  system must borrow the category's grammar while keeping every brand
  illustration original. If everything becomes a reference, the direction
  disappears.

## Direction 3 — Acid Stoa

**Thesis:** hyperpop poster logic. PC Music, deconstructed club flyer,
stretched type, gel-render blobs. The loudest direction — cute the way a
100 gecs cover is cute: abrasive, saturated, sincere underneath.

- **Palette:** acid green `#B6FF3C`, ultraviolet `#7A2BFF`, signal white,
  near-black `#101014`. One accent per post, never all three.
- **Type:** Inter Black stretched/condensed to the point of distortion for
  quotes; Silkscreen or another pixel face for metadata, laid out like a
  render's debug text.
- **Motifs:** gel-render 3D blobs, chrome ribbons, warped grids, busts melted
  into goo, oversized cursors and UI fragments. Glitch used structurally,
  not as a filter.
- **Composition:** asymmetric, tension-forward; type can break the frame or
  wrap objects. Each post looks like a poster, the grid looks like a rave
  archive.
- **Caption voice:** lowercase, clipped, faintly unhinged sincerity.
  "epictetus dropped this in 108 ad and we are still not ready."
- **Sample post:** "Waste no more time arguing what a good man should be. Be
  one." — type stretched to the bleed, a single perfect chrome sphere sitting
  in the counter of the O.
- **Risk:** hardest to keep legible at thumbnail size; the style rewards
  confidence and punishes hedging.

## Direction 4 — The Institute

**Thesis:** the seriously-unserious one. Play it completely straight:
museum placard, catalog number, footnotes, archival layout — but the
"artifact" being catalogued is unbearably cute. A pink blob in a toga,
accession no. CS-0041, "Blob (Contemplating Fate), artist unknown." The
comedy is entirely in the deadpan.

- **Palette:** archival grey `#E8E6E1`, off-black `#1A1A1A`, safety orange
  `#FF5A1F` as the sole accent (catalog numbers, underlines). Clinical,
  flat, lots of white space.
- **Type:** Space Grotesk or Familjen Grotesk for everything — placard
  hierarchy: quote as the "work title," author as "artist," translator and
  passage as the medium line ("Ink on inevitability, c. 170 AD").
- **Motifs:** one cute object per post, museum-photographed on grey: blob
  philosophers, a plush hourglass, a tiny marble column with a bow on it.
  Measurement ticks, "do not touch" iconography, condition reports.
- **Composition:** rigid grid, generous margins, the cute object small and
  perfectly lit. Restraint is the whole gag.
- **Caption voice:** dry catalog copy that slowly reveals feeling.
  "CS-0041. Acquired following the donor's realization that most of her
  worries were, upon inspection, not her department."
- **Sample post:** "You have power over your mind — not outside events." —
  set as a placard beside a photographed pink blob gazing at a wall.
- **Risk:** the quietest grid of the four; it grows on people rather than
  grabbing them. Requires consistent "photography" lighting on the renders.

## How to choose

- Want maximum contrast between form and content → **Chrome Tears** or
  **Acid Stoa** (loud cute vs. severe text).
- Want the warmest, most tactile on-ramp and room for physical-world posts →
  **The Sticker Drawer**.
- Want the joke to be tone rather than decoration, and the lowest per-post
  production cost → **The Institute**.

Current read: **The Sticker Drawer** has the strongest emotional hook and the
best bridge between designed posts and Mara's real life. **The Institute**
remains the most restrained alternative; Chrome Tears is the trend play; Acid
Stoa is the taste play. The next decision is not whether to mix directions, but
which expression of The Sticker Drawer — flat character art, tactile sparkle,
analog collage, or restrained editorial — becomes the default.
