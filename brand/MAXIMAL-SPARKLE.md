# Cutesy Stoic — Maximal Sparkle

A production specification for the dense, tactile expression of **The Sticker
Drawer**.

## One-line definition

A completely serious Stoic quotation sits in a protected clearing at the
center of a beloved 1990s sticker-book page, while dozens of original glitter,
foil, and printed stickers accumulate exuberantly around it.

## Why it works

The quote is not made cute. It remains typographically calm, black, and
bookish. The stickers supply all of the noise, nostalgia, and emotional
openness. That separation creates the Cutesy Stoic tension: the philosophy is
treated with respect, but the page around it has clearly been decorated by
someone incapable of leaving a good thing unstickered.

The abundance is important. This is not a quote card with a few decorative
icons. It should feel collected, handled, traded, and added to over time.

## Format

- **Canvas:** 1080×1350 px, portrait 4:5
- **Background:** one continuous, full-bleed sheet of pale warm-white paper
- **No outer object:** no card, rounded corners, border, frame, or cast-shadowed
  sheet
- **Texture:** subtle paper fibers and natural tonal variation; clean and
  fairly bright, never sepia
- **Thumbnail test:** the quotation must remain readable when the post is
  reduced to approximately 180 px wide

## Composition

### The clearing

Reserve an irregular central clearing for the quotation. It should occupy
roughly 48–58% of the canvas width and 34–44% of its height. The clearing is not
a box or a visible shape; it exists because the stickers stop just before the
letterforms.

Stickers may enter the spaces between lines or approach the punctuation, but
they may never cover a letter. The clearing should feel protected rather than
mechanically masked.

### Sticker density

Target **25–35 stickers**:

- 4–6 large anchors
- 8–12 medium subjects
- 12–20 tiny fillers and sparkle marks

Density increases toward the perimeter. Spacing is irregular. Some stickers
nearly touch; others leave small pockets of bare paper. Avoid even distribution,
radial symmetry, or a digital icon-grid rhythm.

A few objects may crop slightly at an edge, but the page should still feel
composed rather than wallpapered.

### Attribution

The attribution and catalog code sit along the bottom edge in one small,
quiet line:

`CS–0001 · EPICTETUS · ENCHIRIDION 1`

Use the next sequential catalog number for each post. Attribution remains
legible and is never converted into a sticker.

## Typography

- **Quote:** Century Schoolbook, Newsreader, or a similarly sober literary
  serif
- **Case:** sentence case
- **Color:** soft black, approximately `#171717`
- **Treatment:** no outline, shadow, gradient, arch, distortion, or sticker
  border
- **Alignment:** centered by default; soft asymmetry is permitted when it
  improves the clearing
- **Leading:** generous enough to let small stickers approach between lines
  without damaging readability
- **Metadata:** narrow, plain grotesque or monospaced sans in small caps

Avoid bubbly display type. The typography carries the serious half of the
identity.

## Sticker illustration language

Every sticker is original, even when the category language is nostalgic.

- Simple, slightly awkward silhouettes
- Uncomplicated black outlines
- Tiny dot eyes and economical expressions
- Flat, cheerful color beneath the finish
- Ordinary subjects treated as worthy of collecting
- Mild scale inconsistency and hand-placed rotation
- No single recurring mascot is required

The collection may include animals, fruit, weather, toys, flowers, school
supplies, household objects, celestial forms, food, transport, and abstract
hearts or stars. Roughly 20–30% of the subjects may echo the passage; the rest
should feel associatively chosen or simply loved.

Do not reproduce, trace, or closely imitate an identifiable vintage sticker.
Borrow the era's material and compositional grammar, not its characters.

## Materials and physical realism

Use a mixed collection rather than applying one digital glitter texture to
everything.

Suggested balance:

- 35–45% prismatic or holographic foil
- 20–30% translucent glitter
- 15–20% pearlescent or iridescent film
- 10–20% flat printed paper

Each finish should react differently to light. Preserve tiny scratches,
confetti variation, print grain, occasional misregistration, and slight wear.
The result should look photographed, not rendered as glossy 3D plastic.

### White die-cut border

The white sticker backing is essential.

- Cleaner and approximately 8–12% lighter than the background paper
- Wide enough to read clearly at feed size
- Slightly irregular around illustrated shapes
- Defined by tonal contrast and a tiny contact shadow, not a dark outline
- Physically thin: no puffy vinyl, foam-board depth, or heavy drop shadow

This contrast is what makes the stickers look placed *on* the page rather than
printed into it.

## Color

The paper and typography remain fixed while the sticker palette changes by
post.

- **Paper:** pale warm white, approximately `#F5F2EA`
- **Sticker backing:** cleaner soft white, approximately `#FFFDF8`
- **Type:** off-black, approximately `#171717`

Sticker colors may span the full cheerful spectrum. The collection should not
be forced into a restrained brand palette, but adjacent large stickers should
not compete at identical hue and value. Use tiny metallic stars and gems to
bridge color gaps.

## Quote selection

This treatment works best with a short quotation that can remain large:

- Usually 5–16 words
- Prefer one complete sentence
- Three to five display lines
- Strong enough to withstand visual abundance
- Public-domain translation, fully verified before production

Long passages belong in the sparse Sticker Drawer layout, not here.

## Caption voice

The image is maximal; the caption stays plain.

- Begin with Mara's one-sentence reading of the passage
- Add source and translator information
- Optional final deadpan line, used sparingly

Examples:

- \"you get a sticker for noticing.\"
- \"today's sticker for coping appropriately.\"
- \"no prize for postponing, unfortunately.\"

Never use baby talk or make the quotation the punchline.

## Allowed variation

The following can change without creating a new style:

- Sticker subjects
- Balance of finish families
- Anchor-sticker placement
- Quote alignment
- Clearing size within the prescribed range
- Overall color temperature of the sticker collection
- One or two passage-related visual motifs

## Not allowed

- Modern kawaii or chibi philosophers
- Glossy puffy 3D stickers
- Y2K chrome or vaporwave effects
- Airbrushed rainbow fantasy
- Emoji or app-icon styling
- Branded, licensed, or recognizable vintage characters
- Evenly spaced icon grids
- A visible text box, card, or speech bubble
- Stickers covering the quotation
- Fake sepia aging
- One uniform glitter overlay across every material
- Additional slogans embedded in stickers

## Repeatable production recipe

1. Choose and verify a short public-domain quote.
2. Set the quote and attribution before adding any art.
3. Establish the invisible central clearing.
4. Place 4–6 large anchor stickers around the perimeter.
5. Add medium subjects with varied scale and rotation.
6. Fill awkward gaps with tiny stars, gems, dots, and abstract sparkles.
7. Balance material finishes rather than only colors.
8. Make every die-cut border cleaner white than the paper.
9. Add minimal contact shadows and physical imperfections.
10. Check the post at thumbnail size.
11. Confirm that no letter is obscured and no referenced vintage design was
    copied.
12. Export at 1080×1350.

## Prompt skeleton

> Create a portrait 4:5 Cutesy Stoic post on full-bleed pale warm-white paper.
> Set the exact verified quotation in large sober black schoolbook serif within
> an invisible central clearing. Surround it organically with 25–35 original
> 1980s/90s-style die-cut stickers in varied scales and mixed prismatic,
> holographic, glitter, pearlescent, and flat printed finishes. Make the sticker
> backing a cleaner white than the paper with a tiny natural contact shadow.
> Preserve irregular hand placement, tactile surface wear, and abundant
> color. Do not cover any letter. No card, frame, branded character, modern
> kawaii, puffy 3D, or additional text.
